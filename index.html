Prologue

Not the beginning I expected

A late enrollee’s first page.

I come from Angeles City, Pampanga. My academic path began at Angeles University Foundation and continued at Our Lady of Fatima University, Pampanga, where I completed my Medical Technology journey. In March 2026, I passed the Medical Technologist Licensure Examination and earned the title of Registered Medical Technologist. Beyond academics, I am a singer at night and an aspiring physician in the morning.

On July 30, I entered my first Biochemistry class already behind on the lessons covered in earlier sessions and walked directly into two quizzes. I had spent the days before catching up on missed lessons and activities, not only in Biochemistry but in my other subjects as well. With little to no sleep, I had barely any time left to prepare and only a faint idea of what to expect. Yet that moment became more than an unexpected assessment. It became proof that the Medical Technologist I had already become would remain part of the physician I am now becoming.

This portfolio follows that growth: from relying on what I once knew to understanding why it matters; from releasing laboratory results to interpreting their place in a patient’s story; and from memorizing details to discovering the wonder of the human body as one connected system.

With God’s grace, even an unexpected beginning can become a meaningful one.

NMAT examination

A sign to keep going.

I took the NMAT on October 19, 2025, and when I received a score that exceeded 40, I chose to see it as more than just a number. At a time when I was still questioning whether medicine was truly the path I was meant to take, it felt like a sign to keep going.

06March
2026

Medical Technologist Licensure Examination

A title, and a clearer purpose.

A few months later, on March 6, 2026, I passed the Medical Technologist Licensure Examination. Passing the MTLE was another meaningful milestone for me—not only because I earned the title of a licensed medical technologist, but because it made me pause and reflect on what I truly wanted from my career in healthcare.

As a medical technologist, I have learned to appreciate the quiet but essential role the laboratory plays in patient care. I learned to be precise, patient, and accountable because behind every tube of blood, every specimen, and every result is a person waiting for an answer. There is something deeply meaningful about knowing that my work can contribute to someone’s diagnosis and treatment, even when I may never meet the patient.

Yet, with experience, I began to recognize something within myself. I did not want to remain only at the point where the answers were generated. I wanted to understand the person who needed those answers. I wanted to sit with a patient, hear their story, put the pieces together, and understand how the laboratory findings fit into the bigger picture of their health.

That realization made me look differently at my role as a medical technologist. Rather than seeing medicine as a departure from the profession I worked hard to enter, I began to see it as the next step in a journey that had already given me a strong foundation. My experiences in the laboratory have taught me the value of evidence, accuracy, and the science behind disease. More importantly, they have shown me that every result has a human story behind it.

This is why I want to pursue medicine. I want to take what I have learned from behind the laboratory bench and bring it closer to the patient. I want the responsibility of not only understanding the science, but also listening, reasoning, diagnosing, and helping someone navigate what comes next.

The NMAT and my success in the MTLE felt like two moments that reaffirmed a desire I had been carrying for some time. I choose to see them as signs—not because they made the decision for me, but because they gave me the courage to recognize what I already felt.

“My journey began in the laboratory. Now, I want to take that knowledge further and become a physician who can help find, interpret, and act upon the answers that matter most to a patient.

PAUSE?

Multiple class suspensions

The classes paused. The learning did not.

Online lectures and asynchronous tasks required me to take greater ownership of my learning. As someone who learns slowly, I had to work through each topic carefully and by heart. I am especially thankful for Doc A’s high-yield lectures, which helped me find structure when the usual rhythm of class was interrupted.

110questions

Long examination results

When preparation felt powerless, faith carried me through.

I entered the long examination prepared, yet once it began, it felt as though every weapon I had brought into the room had stopped working. My pen, memory, questionnaire, and Scantron no longer felt like allies in the same battle.

Before submitting my examination, I prayed, “Lord, I surrender everything to You.”

I left the room disheartened and uncertain whether I would pass. Speaking with friends who had struggled too reminded me that I was not alone. We acknowledged how difficult the examination had been, and I began thinking about the next best steps I could take to improve before the Prelim examination.

When the results were released on Friday, God’s grace met me again. I had passed and exceeded my own projected score of 30 to 40 out of 110. The result showed me that I understood more than fear had allowed me to believe, while also making the next lesson clear: understanding is only the beginning, and I still need deeper mastery to achieve a stronger result in the Prelim examination.

Prelim reflection

Becoming is happening in the details.

This Prelim period did not unfold smoothly. I arrived late, met unexpected quizzes, adjusted to suspended classes, encountered a new virtual laboratory, and learned to study through uncertainty. Yet each experience showed me that growth is not always loud. Sometimes it looks like returning to a difficult chapter. Sometimes it is a page of notes, a careful question, or the decision to keep learning when the classroom is unavailable.

My Medical Technology background remains one of my strengths, but Medicine is already asking me to stretch beyond it, to connect the result to the mechanism, the mechanism to the symptoms, and all of it to the human being in front of me. Biochemistry is beginning to change how I see the body: not as isolated facts, but as countless small processes working together to make life possible.

“I am not only learning what the body is made of. I am learning to see why every tiny part matters to the person I hope to care for.”

Midterms · Prologue

A deeper chapter of becoming

Beyond familiar names.
Into the pathways of life.

My Midterm journey asked me to look beyond the Biochemistry I knew from pre-med. ATP and some pathways sounded familiar, but the principles behind them were far deeper than I had understood. Bioenergetics, biologic oxidation, and the respiratory chain challenged me to connect energy, electron transfer, and cellular work.

Rainy days shifted some classes online. A full schedule of activities and personal struggles made it difficult to manage my time. Biochemistry became one of my hardest subjects, but I kept trying: returning to the book, participating in discussions, and building connections one topic at a time.

There were encouraging moments, too: my first highest-scorer recognition in our Messenger group, our commended G6PD infographic, and the familiar joy of working through a virtual laboratory as a Medical Technologist. This chapter records both the difficulty and the determination to continue.

With God’s grace, I keep learning, even when understanding takes time.

Midterm reflection

A Dream Worth Fighting For:
My Biochemistry Reflection

Medical school is not always full of rainbows and butterflies. Sometimes, it means sleepless nights, overwhelming lessons, disappointing scores, and moments when I question if I am truly good enough. Biochemistry has taught me that pursuing my dream will not always be easy, and I am learning to be okay with that.

There are moments when I look around and see people who seem to understand everything so effortlessly, and I cannot help but wonder if I am doing enough. But I remind myself that not being the best in the room does not mean I do not belong in it. I am here for a reason. I worked, prayed, and persevered to get here. This was once just a dream I could only imagine, and now I am blessed enough to live it. I refuse to let a difficult subject, a disappointing score, or a moment of self-doubt make me forget how far I have come.

I am also grateful that I do not have to go through this journey alone. My boyfriend, who is also my best friend, and my mom are my two one-call-away people—the ones I can turn to when everything feels too heavy. Whether I need someone to listen, comfort me, remind me of my worth, or satisfy my random food cravings in the middle of stressful days, they always find ways to make life feel a little lighter. They remind me that even while chasing a big dream, I am allowed to rest, to feel overwhelmed, and to lean on the people who love me. Their presence is one of God’s sweetest reminders that I do not have to carry everything on my own.

I know there is still so much for me to learn, not only in Biochemistry but in every major subject that awaits me. I have a responsibility to myself and to the future physician I hope to become to keep reaching for higher heights and give my best in everything I do. I may not always get things right the first time, but I can always choose to learn, try again, and become better than I was yesterday.

Above all, I hold on to my faith. Through God’s grace, I believe I will make it through even the most difficult days. I may still have moments when I doubt myself, but I trust that God will continue to guide me as long as I do my part. I have my grit, passion, perseverance, and resilience, and I am learning that these qualities matter just as much as the grades I work so hard to achieve.

This is not me claiming that I am better than everyone else. This is me learning to believe in myself, even on the days when doing so feels difficult. I can acknowledge that I still have room to grow without questioning whether I deserve to be here. I do not need to be the smartest person in the room to prove that I am capable of becoming a good physician.

Biochemistry is just one chapter of a much bigger journey, and I know there will be more challenges ahead. But whenever things get hard, I want to remember that the little girl who once dreamed of becoming a doctor would be proud to see me here—still trying, still learning, and still choosing to move forward.

Medical school may not always be beautiful, but this dream is still worth fighting for. With God’s grace, my mom’s unwavering love, my boyfriend’s comforting presence, and the determination I carry within myself, I will keep showing up, keep believing, and keep giving my best. I belong here, I have what it takes to keep going, and someday, I will look back on these difficult days and thank myself for never giving up.

Sophia Angelica Mae R. Salas, RMTMD-1 · College of Medicine
